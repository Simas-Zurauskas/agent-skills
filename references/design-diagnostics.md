# Product-context design diagnostics

Use these questions to find defects and missed opportunities, not to enforce one studio style. Owner decisions and
product requirements outrank a critic's taste. No font family, hue, saturation level, hero, list, card, phone mockup,
three-surface stack or style genre is globally required or forbidden. A single good text face can be enough.
Historical examples in lessons, craft and vernacular references describe their original product; their bans and
numeric caps do not automatically apply here. Do not turn palette statistics or aesthetic scores into gates.

- **Product fit:** what user task, product truth or explicit art direction explains this choice? Conventional UI is
  useful where familiarity matters; an unusual device needs to serve the product too.
- **Content and function:** does every direction retain the same required strings, data, blocks and meaningful
  controls? Are imagery, rankings and metaphors consistent with the product's actual rules?
- **Hierarchy:** can a viewer find the next action and important information? A list may be the right hierarchy.
  A hero, surface depth or imagery is useful only when this flow needs it.
- **Typography:** does the face render correctly, fit real strings and carry the intended tone? Check weights,
  numeral alignment where needed and actual glyph support. Familiar fonts are not failures by name.
- **Color and surfaces:** are relationships legible and intentional? A quiet neutral system can be the correct
  direction. Saturation and surface count are diagnostic descriptions, not targets.
- **Distinctness:** can the owner see meaningful alternatives in structure, type, density, imagery, interaction
  emphasis or color? Compare nearest pairs. Name their real differences; palette-only variation can be valid when
  that is the requested question. Do not violate owner constraints to manufacture a missing axis.
- **Craft:** inspect true-size renders, overflow, truncation, image quality, consistent spacing, ordinary control
  usability and exact content. Apply target-specific checks and only relevant platform requirements.
- **Coherence:** does the board describe what the screens actually show? Remove only an accessory that weakens the
  result, not a required block or useful familiar component. No compulsory removal quota.

Critiques use evidence and severity, not mandatory scores. A substantive issue affects required content, usability,
rendering, the requested design distinction or a stated owner/product constraint. Preference-only changes can be
recorded without causing another build. Do not add accessibility, motion or edge-state scope beyond the task's
applicable requirements; do preserve ordinary usable controls, readable labels and existing behavior.
