## 2. Per-source rules (concrete)

### 2.1 NN/G — UX foundations and AI-generated UI

**10 heuristics (#1, unchanged since 1994).** The ones an agent most often violates, with the source wording:
- #1 Visibility of system status: "keep users informed … through appropriate feedback within a reasonable amount of time." Loading, saving, success and error states must exist.
- #3 User control and freedom: "clearly marked 'emergency exit'". Undo/cancel/back.
- #4 Consistency and standards: Jakob's Law — "people spend most of their time using digital products other than yours."
- #5 Error prevention: eliminate error-prone conditions or confirm before commit.
- #6 Recognition over recall: labels and options visible, not remembered.
- #8 Aesthetic and minimalist design: "Every extra unit of information … competes with the relevant units of information and diminishes their relative visibility." Does NOT mean flat design.
- #9 Errors in plain language, "precisely indicate the problem, and constructively suggest a solution", no error codes.

**AI prototyping study (#2, #3) — what AI output gets wrong (use as a failure catalogue).**
- "Frankenstein layout": randomly pieced-together page with (a) unnecessary visual clutter, (b) repeated design elements (same info shown twice, e.g. progress bars duplicated), (c) counterintuitive content flow (general to specific broken, e.g. a profile widget in the middle of a sequence), (d) visually prominent containers with low information density (huge card with one number; secondary metric made most prominent so hierarchy contradicts task priority).
- Even with a detailed prompt: "lack of visual hierarchy or grouping among related elements; overused colors creating visual tension; poor color contrast; inconsistent margin spacing". NN/G: these are "the difference between a design that feels thoughtful and one that feels 'almost there'." Verdict: "good from afar, but far from good."
- Generic look: "sans-serif typeface and minimalistic styling … default to component libraries like Shadcn and … Tailwind"; "neutral, minimalistic visual aesthetics with bright color accents … lacks visual identity and emotion"; "flat and interchangeable".
- Wrong pattern from ambiguous words: "profile page" built as a social-media profile. Disambiguate vocabulary.
- Pattern-matching "drives them toward the most common solutions, not the most contextually meaningful ones."

**NN/G prompting remedies (#3) — five strategies, directly usable in a brief:**
1. Precise visual keywords: name an established style (neobrutalism, glassmorphism, Y2K) instead of "simple, clean, modern". Their ranking: "Design a trendy conference landing page" (poor) < "high contrast, blocky layouts, bold colors" (acceptable) < "in a neobrutalist style" (best). Do not say "design like Apple/Airbnb" — "Famous brands don't guarantee good design."
2. Lightweight visual references: moodboards, inspiration images, design-system screenshots. Managing expectations: even then "unlikely … pixel-perfect"; Material 3 screenshot gave similar tone with "more saturated colors". For precision, connect to tokens (Figma MCP) rather than images.
3. Visual analysis step: have a chatbot describe a reference page's style in words, then convert to prompt keywords.
4. Realistic mock data (content-first): "working with realistic data so that visual design supports the key content".
5. Attach code snippets (own components, open-source design systems): most precise, but long snippets overload context.
- Also: "don't confuse visual fidelity with design quality." And "Good design decisions can't be automated … thinking through design requirements, weighing tradeoffs."

**GenUI vs vibe coding (#4):** vague prompt ("Build me a trip planner") leaves layout, interaction patterns and hierarchy to the AI; a specific one makes the AI "executing a designer's vision". The agent is accountable for "execution fidelity" when a spec exists and for "design judgment" when it does not — so the skill must force the spec step.

**State of UX 2026 (#5):** "UI is cheaper to produce, due to standardization … If you're just slapping together components from a design system, you're already replaceable by AI. What isn't easy to automate? Curated taste, research-informed contextual understanding, critical thinking". 2026 "year of AI fatigue": "Lazy AI features and AI slop are now ubiquitous"; "When everything gets an AI sparkle, it becomes noise, not novelty." (Implication: no sparkle icons, no "AI-powered" badges.)

**Taste (#7):** "designers exhibit taste by being intentional in the choices they make. All details should work to expand and enhance a single organizing thought, idea, or vision." Taste includes "a strategy" for orchestrating hundreds of small decisions around one vision.

**Animation duration (#8):** most animations 100–500 ms; "look for the shortest time that an animation can take without being jarring. It is far more common for animations to be too long than too short." Simple feedback (checkbox/toggle) ~100 ms; modal into view 200–300 ms; 500 ms "start to feel like a real drag"; 100–400 ms appropriate, 400 only for large movements; entering elements slightly longer than exiting.

**Liquid Glass critique (#9):** text over translucent layers is hard to read; "anything placed on top of something else becomes harder to see"; animated controls that "insist on animating themselves" turn delight into distraction by the tenth use; crowded tap targets violate "at least 0.4 cm between targets (and 1 cm x 1 cm tap areas)". Lesson: do not reproduce glass or decorative motion because a platform vendor did.

### 2.2 Laws of UX (#10) and Baymard (#11)

- Doherty Threshold: give system feedback within 400 ms; use perceived performance (skeletons, progress) when slower; progress bars make waits tolerable "regardless of their accuracy".
- Headline laws an agent can apply as procedures: Hick (decision time grows with number/complexity of choices — cap visible options; Impeccable's rubric uses ≤4 at a decision point, 5–7 borderline, 8+ overload, citing Cowan 2001 as revision of Miller's 7±2); Fitts (size and distance of targets); Proximity / Common Region / Similarity / Uniform Connectedness (grouping by space, boundary, likeness, connection); Jakob's Law; Aesthetic-Usability Effect (pleasing design is perceived as more usable — a reason polish matters, not a licence to hide usability flaws); Von Restorff (the one different element is remembered — so only one primary action should differ); Peak-End; Postel's law for inputs.
- Baymard: average checkout 2024 = 5.1 steps, 11.3 form fields; 17% abandoned due to checkout complexity (their quant insight GC050); "what really matters … is the number of form fields". Forms: labels above fields, not inline; placeholders "shouldn't be used for the primary label" but are good for format examples; label must stay visible while typing so errors are correctable; avoid extensive multi-column field layouts (their 2023 article: 16% of sites make this mistake); credit-card number field must allow and auto-format spaces (80% of sites don't); explain why a phone field is required (14% abandon if phone is simply required). Relevance to us: RSVP, guest-count and gift forms — fewest fields, labels above, explain every non-obvious required field.

### 2.3 Refactoring UI (#12, secondary digest of the book)

Rules stated as procedures (numbers are the digest's):
- Start with a feature, not a layout; grayscale first, add colour last (forces hierarchy from size/weight/space).
- Hierarchy: "Deliberately de-emphasize most things so the few important things win." Use weight and colour, not size alone: 2–3 text colours, 2 weights (400/500 and 600/700). Never use lighter than 400 weight on screens. Emphasise by de-emphasising siblings. One primary action per screen (solid), secondary (outline/soft), tertiary (link). Destructive is not automatically big-and-red.
- Labels are a last resort; fold label into value ("12 left in stock"); style labels quiet.
- Systems before screens: type scale, spacing scale, grey scale, shadow scale, colour shades, one radius scale. "If you're ever nudging by 1px, you're missing a system." Spacing scale with visible ratio steps (~≥25%): 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128. Type scale hand-picked (12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 30, 36, 48), px/rem not em.
- White space: start with too much, then remove. Do not fill the screen: a 480–640 px form column on a wide canvas is correct. Grouping: "closer within than between"; fix ambiguous spacing before adding borders.
- Line length 45–75 characters; line-height proportional to size and width (small text / wide columns up to ~2.0; large headlines ~1.1–1.3); tighten letter-spacing on big headlines, widen on ALL-CAPS labels; right-align numbers in tables; baseline-align mixed sizes.
- Colour: need 8–10 greys, 5–10 shades of primary and of each accent, defined up front. Don't make shades by lightness alone (adjust saturation, rotate hue slightly). Greys carry a temperature (cool or warm) used everywhere. Flip contrast for accessible badges (dark text on light tint). Never colour-only meaning.
- Depth: light from above; ~5-level shadow scale mapped to meaning; two-part shadows (ambient + direct); fewer borders ("border-everything is the #1 cluttered-UI smell") — use shadow, background shift or space.
- Images: "good photos or none"; text over image needs a treatment (darken overlay, etc.); never scale small icons up 3x (put them in a tinted container instead).
- Finishing: supercharge defaults (custom bullets, accent borders), designed empty states, rich components ("a table can embed avatars, stacked primary/secondary text, status pills").
- Method: "steal deliberately" — find a well-designed product solving the same problem and name specifically what it does differently (spacing, shade count, weight contrast).
- Decision defaults (digest): body 16 px; secondary 14; unrelated blocks 24–32 gap, related 8–12; card padding 16–24; one radius family (4–6 px professional or 10–16 px friendly); table rows ≥44–48 px.

### 2.4 Typography: Butterick, Bringhurst, Utopia, Every Layout

**Butterick (#13), verbatim numbers:** "Point size should be 10–12 points in printed documents, 15–25 pixels on the web." "Line spacing should be 120–145% of the point size." "The average line length should be 45–90 characters (including spaces)." Most visible improvement: a professional font; avoid goofy, monospaced (for body), most free, and system fonts "especially Times New Roman and Arial". Curly quotes; bold or italic "as little as possible, and not together"; underline only links; all caps fine for under one line; centered text sparingly; "Use 5–12% extra letterspacing with all caps and small caps"; kerning always on; paragraph separation by first-line indent (1–4x point size) or 4–10 pt space — "Don't use both"; hyphenation always with justified text; proper ellipsis, dashes, apostrophes; non-breaking space after § and ¶.
**Bringhurst (#14, secondary):** ~66 characters per line ideal, 45–75 satisfactory; multi-column 40–50; sans-serif and monospace "ragged-right"; don't go below ~40 characters on justified lines; line-height "variable" depending on face, measure, size. Translate to CSS: `max-width: 66ch` (or 45–75ch), `line-height` set per size.
**Utopia (#15):** define a type scale for a small viewport (example 1.2x at 320 px) and a large one (1.333x at 1500 px) and interpolate with `clamp()`; "H2s are always 'size 4'" instead of per-breakpoint sizes. "Not a substitute for good design, rather it encourages focusing design effort into the creation of a robust system." Same idea for spacing steps.
**Every Layout (#16):** `ch` is "the only appropriate unit" for measure; derive spacing from the text's own dimensions (`em`, `rem`, `ch`) "and your layouts will be beautiful".
**Reconciled starting values for CSS (our synthesis):** body 16–20 px (fluid via clamp), measure 45–75ch (cap at ~65–70ch), body line-height 1.4–1.6 (WCAG 1.4.12 requires layouts to survive 1.5), headings 1.1–1.3, `text-wrap: balance` on headings and `text-wrap: pretty` on paragraphs (Impeccable detector flags orphans/stub lines), heading and ALL-CAPS label tracking +5–12% on caps, slight negative on large display.

### 2.5 Colour systems

- OKLCH (#17): `oklch(L C H)`; L is perceived lightness 0–1 "consistent lightness for our eyes, unlike L in hsl()"; palette can be generated by formula; supports P3; better modifications and a11y because lightness is predictable. Caveats: not every L/C/H combination exists in every gamut (browser falls back); check with a picker. Practical: build tokens in OKLCH, vary L for steps, taper C at extremes, keep H constant per ramp.
- Radix scale (#18): 12 steps with fixed jobs: 1 app background, 2 subtle background, 3 UI element background, 4 hovered, 5 active/selected, 6 subtle borders, 7 borders and focus rings, 8 hovered border, 9 solid backgrounds, 10 hovered solid, 11 low-contrast text, 12 high-contrast text. Usable as a token naming convention: roles, not hues.
- Contrast standards (#19, #20, #21, #28, #26): WCAG 2.2 is the standing requirement — normal text 4.5:1, large text (18 pt or 14 pt bold) 3:1, UI components and graphical objects 3:1 (1.4.11). WCAG 3 (APCA candidate) is "an incomplete draft that will change"; W3C says to prepare by meeting 2.2 now. APCA working targets per secondary sources: Lc 90 small body, Lc 75 regular body minimum, Lc 60 large/bold, Lc 45 very large headings and non-text UI, <Lc 30 should carry no text. Vercel's guideline says "Prefer APCA over WCAG 2 for more accurate perceptual contrast"; Apple HIG names both and gives a table (up to 17 pt, any weight: 4.5:1). Recommended policy: ship WCAG 2.2 AA as the pass/fail gate; use APCA as a second, stricter check for thin/light/dark-mode text.
- Apple: "Make sure all your app's colors work well in light, dark, and increased contrast contexts"; check both appearances.
- Vercel: "Hue consistency. On non-neutral backgrounds, tint borders/shadows/text toward the same hue." "Interactions increase contrast. :hover, :active, :focus have more contrast than rest state." "Set the appropriate color-scheme" and `theme-color` meta.
- Comeau (#27): shadows in transparent black desaturate the surface; colour-match shadows to the background hue (same hue and saturation, lower lightness). All shadows share one light direction (same x:y ratio, e.g. y = 2x); as elevation rises: offset up, blur up, opacity down; layer 2+ shadows.

### 2.6 Accessibility (#20–#22, #26, #28)

WCAG 2.2 AA must-haves with numbers (from the spec text I fetched):
- 1.4.3 contrast 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text 18 pt / 14 pt bold). 1.4.11 non-text contrast 3:1 for UI component states and graphical objects.
- 1.4.10 Reflow: no two-dimensional scrolling at 320 CSS px wide (equals 400% zoom of 1280). 1.4.4 Resize: text to 200% without loss.
- 1.4.12 Text spacing must not break layouts when users set line height ≥1.5x font size, spacing after paragraphs ≥2x font size, letter spacing ≥0.12x, word spacing ≥0.16x (verified in spec text).
- 2.4.7 Focus visible; 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (AA): a focused control must not be entirely hidden by sticky headers/footers/banners (spec: "not entirely hidden due to author-created content"). 2.4.13 Focus Appearance is AAA (2 CSS px perimeter equivalent, 3:1 change) — a good target but not required.
- 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum, AA): at least 24x24 CSS px, or spacing so a 24-px circle on each undersized target doesn't overlap another; exceptions for inline text links, user-agent controls, equivalent control. The spec "encourages" larger. Platform sizes larger: Apple iOS 44x44 pt (28x28 minimum in the table I read), plus about 12 pt padding around bezeled controls, 24 pt around unbezeled; Vercel: ≥24 px, 44 px on mobile; NN/G: 1 cm x 1 cm tap area, 0.4 cm spacing.
- 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA): every drag has a single-pointer alternative (Vercel: "Gestures have alternatives").
- 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide: auto-moving content lasting more than 5 seconds alongside other content needs a pause/stop/hide mechanism (kills auto-scrolling marquees and autoplay carousels).
- 3.3.7 Redundant Entry, 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (don't force a cognitive test; allow paste and password managers), 3.2.6 Consistent Help.
- Reduced motion: Apple HIG — respond to Reduce Motion by "reducing automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion"; Emil — reduced means fewer and gentler, not zero (keep opacity/colour, drop movement); Comeau — `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` collapse transitions.
- Apple: let people enlarge text at least 200%; "Increase Contrast" variant.
- ARIA (#22): "No ARIA is better than Bad ARIA"; "A role is a promise" — `<div role="button">` obliges you to implement keyboard behaviour. Vercel: "Semantics before ARIA", native elements first; follow APG patterns for composite widgets (tabs, dialog, menu, combobox) incl. focus management (move focus in, trap, return focus on close). Accessible names for icon-only buttons (`aria-label`). Hierarchical headings and a skip link.
- Vercel form a11y: every control has a label; errors next to field and focus first error on submit; announce async updates with polite `aria-live`; don't rely on colour alone; `inputmode`/`type`/`autocomplete` correct; input font ≥16 px on mobile (prevents iOS zoom).

### 2.7 Motion and interaction craft (Emil, Rauno, Vercel, Comeau, NN/G)

Emil Kowalski's skill (#23) — the most concrete source:
- Should it animate? by frequency: 100+ times/day (keyboard shortcuts, command palette) = "No animation. Ever."; tens/day (hover, list nav) = remove or drastically reduce; occasional (modals, drawers, toasts) = standard; rare/first-time = may delight. "Never animate keyboard-initiated actions." Purposes that justify motion: spatial consistency, state indication, explanation, feedback, avoiding jarring pop-in. "If the purpose is just 'it looks cool' and the user will see it often, don't animate."
- Easing: entering/exiting = ease-out; on-screen movement = ease-in-out; hover/colour = ease; constant motion = linear; default ease-out. "Never use ease-in for UI animations." Built-in CSS easings are too weak; use custom: `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`; `--ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1)`; drawer `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`.
- Durations: button press 100–160 ms; tooltips/small popovers 125–200; dropdowns/selects 150–250; modals/drawers 200–500; rule: UI animation under 300 ms (modals, drawers, sheets, toasts exempt). "A 180ms select animation feels more responsive than a 400ms one." Exit faster than enter (e.g. 250 in / 200 out). Press slow where deciding (hold-to-delete 2 s linear), release snappy (200 ms ease-out).
- Details: `:active { transform: scale(0.97) }` (range 0.95–0.98) on every pressable; never animate from `scale(0)` — start `scale(0.9–0.95)` plus opacity 0; popovers scale from trigger (`transform-origin`), modals stay centred; tooltips delay first, instant for adjacent ones afterwards; CSS transitions not keyframes for interruptible/rapid UI; `@starting-style` for entry; blur ≤20 px (2 px typical) to mask crossfades; stagger 30–80 ms between items, never block interaction; animate only `transform` and `opacity`; no `transition: all`; hover effects gated by `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`; springs for drag/interruptible gestures, bounce 0.1–0.3 and usually none; drag dismiss on velocity (> ~0.11 px/ms) not only distance; damping at boundaries; pointer capture; ignore extra touches.
- Process: review animations next day, in slow motion (2–5x) and frame-by-frame; test gestures on a real device; match motion to component personality ("A playful component can be bouncier. A professional dashboard should be crisp and fast.").
- Review output format he requires: a Before | After | Why table (useful for our critique step).

Rauno (#25): interaction metaphors reuse; gestures interruptible ("imagine if [flipping a page] were an animation that you had to wait for"); lightweight actions trigger during the swipe, destructive actions only on release; apply scale delta immediately then animate past threshold; spatial consistency (things leave where they came from); "Frequency & Novelty": removed motion from core keyboard interactions in his own app and it "felt faster"; macOS context menus appear without motion but fade out; Fitts's law (corner/edge targets effectively infinite); don't obscure content under a finger (loupe pattern); "fidgetability" as an optional delight.
Vercel (#26) animation rules: "Necessity check. Only animate when it clarifies cause & effect or when it adds deliberate delight"; CSS > WAAPI > JS libraries; compositor-friendly props only; interruptible; correct transform origin; honour reduced motion; autoplay >5 s needs controls; loading spinners need ~150–300 ms show-delay and ~300–500 ms minimum visible time; skeletons mirror final content; mutations <500 ms or optimistic UI with undo.
Comeau (#27): ease-out for entering, ease-in for exiting the viewport; "friendlier and safer" reduced-motion default.
NN/G (#8) numbers are in 2.1. Doherty 400 ms in 2.2.

### 2.8 Platform systems: what is reusable vs platform-specific

Apple HIG 2025 Liquid Glass (#28):
- Reusable principle: separate a functional layer (controls/navigation) from the content layer. "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer." "Use Liquid Glass effects sparingly … Limit these effects to the most important functional elements." "Only use clear Liquid Glass for components that appear over visually rich backgrounds" and add a dark dimming layer ~35% opacity over bright media. Regular variant (more opaque) when there is text. Choose materials by semantic meaning, not apparent colour. Avoid quaternary vibrancy on thin materials (too low contrast). Avoid similar colours in control labels over colourful backgrounds.
- Platform-specific (do not port): the glass material itself, vibrancy APIs, Dynamic Type, 44 pt iOS target size as Apple unit, SF fonts. NN/G's critique (#9) shows the material hurt legibility and tap-target spacing in practice — so for the web treat translucency as optional decoration, never as the carrier of text.
Material 3 Expressive (#29, #30):
- Research: 46 studies, 18,000+ participants; "people really do prefer to feel something": expressive preferred by all ages, up to 87% among 18–24; key elements spotted up to 4x faster (e.g. larger Send button above the keyboard in a secondary colour vs small button in the top bar); older users (45+) as fast as younger; ratings up on modernity (+34%), subculture (+32%), rebelliousness (+30%).
- Its five levers: colour, shape, size, motion, containment — "drawing attention to what matters … making key actions stand out and grouping like elements together". Motion-physics system with spatial springs (position/scale) and effects springs (colour/opacity). 14 updated components.
- Guard rails from the same research: "Context still matters" — a playlist rebuilt as scattered album art "looked modern and exciting, usability scores suffered"; removing text labels from email actions reduced usability; "No amount of expressive design will beat basic functionality"; "a strong minority of users preferred calmer, less intense versions"; expressive designs suffer from lack of familiarity; "Don't compromise your product's core functionality for visual flourishes"; follow accessibility standards. Reusable idea: make the single key action large, high-contrast and placed within thumb reach; use shape/size contrast for hierarchy. Platform-specific: M3 component set, tokens and springs; Android dp units.

### 2.9 Directing AI: designers' advice

- Karri Saarinen, Linear (#33): "For quality, you need a team that views the spec as the baseline, not the finish line." "A great product requires someone … to put more care into it than necessary." "The best design is opinionated" — design for someone in particular. "The simplest way to increase quality is to reduce scope." "Quality is not perfection" but all details must be correct before release. On AI: "While mass production separated the maker's hand from the object, AI attempts to separate the maker's judgment and taste from creation itself … Technology makes it faster to build, but harder to care." Taste = "informed judgment throughout the process"; it "sets constraints, defines standards, and rejects entire categories or paths" — i.e. direction is given before generation, not just filtered after. "Elegance is coherence, restraint, proportion, clarity."
- Loredana Crisan, Figma (#34): taste is care for craft and for the user; she tests for discernment ("Can they articulate what's not working and why?"), empathy, creative energy; "sweating the details most people don't even see: the transition that's a beat too slow, the empty state only a fraction of your users might experience once"; reject the first output ("Doing so would be unacceptable to someone with a strong sense of taste"); critique is how taste forms. Another Figma post (search excerpt only, not fetched): "AI can follow rules, enforce consistency, and even spot visual irregularities, but it can't weigh which of two technically correct solutions creates the most trust or delight."
- Emil (#24): taste "is a trained instinct"; (1) surround yourself with great work, (2) don't label good/bad — "rationalize why something feels great", (3) practise and seek critique. Agent translation: study named references and write down the specific mechanisms.
- NN/G (#3): five prompting strategies in 2.1; specific vocabulary beats adjectives; pair prompt with a reference and real content.
- Vercel (#35): brief = three inputs: Product surface (components, data, actions — "Not 'a dashboard'"), Context of use (who, when, what decision, how much time), Constraints & taste (platform, visual tone, layout, colour systems, responsiveness/accessibility). "Constraints tell v0 what not to invent." Their test: with context of use the output was functional, responsive, mobile-first; without it, "Non-functional search … NOT responsive". Vague prompt took more code and cost than a specific one.
- Anthropic (#31): models sample "from this high-probability center" (Inter, purple gradient on white, minimal animation = "distributional convergence"); fix is direction: name what to avoid and what to prefer, per axis (typography, colour/theme, motion, backgrounds), at the right altitude (not hex codes, not vague). Their type prompt: avoid Inter/Roboto/Open Sans/Lato/system; "High contrast = interesting"; weights 100/200 vs 800/900 rather than 400 vs 600; size jumps 3x+, not 1.5x; "Pick one distinctive font, use it decisively."
- Balyasnikov (#36), a worked agent pipeline: drift across fifty tasks is the failure, not any single output — fix with anchors: principles file, versioned spec, DESIGN.md plus tokens (colour, type, spacing, radii, motion) "strong enough to govern everything built after", references as live links not screenshots (agent can read the real type scale/spacing/focus behaviour/375 px layout), build a demo slice of the system before screens, multi-round review by a reviewer that did not build it, and browser verification. Key rule: "a new element may only enter through the design system … The agent never invents a style inline."
- Impeccable (#32): `init` writes PRODUCT.md (who/what/constraints), `document` writes DESIGN.md; critique scores heuristics and ranks P0 (blocking) to P3; a deterministic detector with 62 rules in three kinds — Source (code/CSS), Browser (rendered page), Design review (needs judgement); the README's own before/after (same model, same brief: "tasteful default: cream page, thin serif, italic accent, pill buttons" vs a page "built around one picture"). Notably it flags the "tasteful default" (cream + italic serif) as slop too — i.e. avoiding purple/Inter is not enough; the substitute defaults also become templates.

### 2.10 Impeccable slop catalogue (#32) — the checkable list, grouped

Source-level (static code/CSS): side-tab accent border on cards; border-accent on rounded element; hairline border plus wide shadow; hard offset shadow (≥4 px unblurred); gradient text; purple/cyan "AI palette"; dark mode with glowing accents; radial-gradient halo / soft spotlight; repeating-gradient stripes; decorative grid-line background; gray text on coloured background; cream/beige as default; overused fonts (Inter, Geist); flat type hierarchy; crushed letter-spacing; tiny interface text; all-caps body; justified text; label/eyebrow above heading; badge pill above hero headline; icon tile stacked above heading; italic serif display headline; oversized hero headline; tiny numbered section labels; monotonous spacing (equal gaps everywhere); nested cards; pulsing status dot; auto-scrolling marquee; bounce/elastic easing; same text repeated in one container; em-dash overuse; generic marketing claims ("supercharge", "world-class"); forced contrast ("Not a feature. A platform."); broken/placeholder images; images hidden under overlays; font/colour/radius/size outside DESIGN.md.
Browser-level (rendered): line length too long (their advice 65–75 characters); content overflowing; text covered by another element; clipped menus/popovers; cramped padding; body text touching page edge; cards flush against scroller edge; unbalanced opening columns; heading closer to previous section than its own content; JS errors on load; content stuck hidden if animation fails; decorative blinking cursor.
Judgement-level: glassmorphism everywhere; extreme card radii; rough SVG illustrations; hero-metric layout; identical card grids; single font for everything.
Their own caveat: "A finding is a reason to look closer: consider the page, the task, and any intentional design choices."

## 3. For our anti-slop skill

### 3A. Quality floor — always get right (a failure here is a defect, not a style choice)

Accessibility
1. Normal text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, UI component boundaries/state indicators and icons that convey meaning ≥3:1, in light AND dark. `[CODE]` (WCAG 1.4.3, 1.4.11, Apple HIG; add APCA ≥Lc 75 body / 60 large as a second check, Vercel)
2. Visible focus ring on every focusable element via `:focus-visible`, ≥3:1 against its surroundings, not hidden by sticky headers/footers. `[BOTH]` (WCAG 2.4.7, 2.4.11, 2.4.13 AAA; Vercel)
3. Whole UI operable by keyboard; native elements first (`button`, `a`, `label`); any `role=` fulfils its keyboard contract; dialogs trap and return focus; composite widgets follow APG. `[BOTH]` (APG, Vercel)
4. Pointer targets ≥24x24 CSS px (WCAG minimum), and ≥44 px on touch/mobile; spacing between small targets. `[CODE]` (WCAG 2.5.8, Apple 44 pt, Vercel, NN/G)
5. No layout break at 320 px width and at 200% text zoom; line-height 1.5 and 0.12em letter-spacing user overrides don't clip. `[CODE]` (WCAG 1.4.10, 1.4.4, 1.4.12)
6. `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured: remove movement/scale/parallax, keep opacity/colour; nothing auto-moves >5 s without pause. `[CODE]` (WCAG 2.2.2; Apple HIG; Emil; Vercel)
7. Every input has a visible `<label>` (not placeholder-only), correct `type`/`inputmode`/`autocomplete`, mobile input font ≥16 px, errors next to the field with focus moved to the first error, text + icon not colour alone. `[CODE]` (Vercel, Baymard, WCAG 3.3.x)
8. Icon-only buttons have accessible names; headings are hierarchical (no skipped levels); one `<h1>`; language attribute set; skip link on multi-section pages; never disable zoom or paste. `[CODE]` (Vercel, Impeccable P3)
9. Gestures/drags have a click/keyboard alternative. `[BOTH]` (WCAG 2.5.7)

States and flows
10. Every interactive element has designed default, hover (pointer-fine only), focus-visible, active/pressed, disabled, and loading states; hover/active/focus raise contrast over rest. `[BOTH]` (Vercel, Emil)
11. Every data view has empty, sparse, dense/long-content, loading (stable skeleton) and error states; no dead-end screens; destructive actions confirm or offer undo; feedback within 400 ms or a progress indicator. `[BOTH]` (NN/G #1/#3/#5/#9, Vercel, Laws of UX Doherty)
12. Errors in plain language that say what happened and how to fix it; no codes. `[VISUAL]` (NN/G #9)
13. Test with real, long and short content (long names, no photo, 0 and 200 guests, long Lithuanian words and diacritics ąčęėįšųūž render correctly in the chosen face — project note, see memory "LT font render check"). `[BOTH]`

Typography, spacing, colour systems
14. Body measure ≤~75ch (target 45–75, ideal ~66), body size 16–20 px, body line-height 1.4–1.6, headings 1.1–1.3, no justified text without hyphenation, no all-caps body, no stub last lines (`text-wrap: balance`/`pretty`), curly quotes and real ellipsis/dashes, tabular numbers in comparisons. `[CODE]` (Butterick, Bringhurst, Refactoring UI, Vercel, Impeccable)
15. Values come from a system: one type scale, one spacing scale (multiplicative steps), one radius family, one shadow scale, one set of colour tokens; no one-off 13 px / odd greys. Nested radii: child ≤ parent. `[CODE]` (Refactoring UI, Vercel, Balyasnikov, Impeccable "outside DESIGN.md")
16. Tokens defined as colour roles (background / subtle bg / element bg / border / solid / text-low / text-high, like Radix' 12 jobs), built in OKLCH with constant hue per ramp; dark mode designed, not inverted; `color-scheme` set. `[CODE]` (Radix, Evil Martians, Vercel)
17. Animate only `transform`/`opacity`; never `transition: all`; no ease-in for UI; UI durations 100–300 ms (modals/drawers up to ~500); interruptible (transitions not keyframes) for rapidly retriggered UI; content visible by default if the animation fails. `[CODE]` (Emil, NN/G, Vercel, Impeccable)
18. Layout integrity: no horizontal overflow, no text covered by another layer, no clipped popovers, images have dimensions (no CLS), safe areas respected. `[CODE]` (Vercel, Impeccable browser checks)
19. Hierarchy: exactly one primary action per view; secondary visibly quieter; content priority matches visual prominence (don't make a secondary metric the biggest thing on the page). `[VISUAL]` (Refactoring UI, NN/G Frankenstein)
20. No duplicated information or components on one screen; logical general-to-specific order; no containers that spend large area on one trivial number. `[BOTH]` (NN/G Frankenstein layout)

### 3B. Craft ceiling — what separates designed from generated

Direction before pixels
1. Write a brief before any UI: product surface (actual components, data, actions), context of use (who, when, what decision, how much time), constraints and taste (platform, tone, layout, colour, a11y). Resolve ambiguous nouns ("profile page" for whom?). `[VISUAL]` (Vercel v0, NN/G #3, Impeccable PRODUCT.md)
2. Name a specific, established visual language or era with real references (live URLs, not adjectives like "clean/modern/simple"; don't say "like Apple"); record what each reference does specifically (spacing, shade count, weight contrast, motion), then derive your own — "steal deliberately", don't clone a brand. `[VISUAL]` (NN/G #3, Refactoring UI, Emil, Balyasnikov)
3. State one organising idea / point of view for the project and an explicit "will not do" list (anti-references) before generating; every later choice should serve it. Opinionated for a specific audience beats flexible for everyone. `[VISUAL]` (NN/G #7, Saarinen, Anthropic, Impeccable)
4. Produce a written design system file (tokens + usage rules, e.g. DESIGN.md) and a demo slice before screens; new elements may only enter via the system; extend the system first. `[CODE]` (Balyasnikov, Impeccable)
5. Use real or realistically generated content (names, dates, Lithuanian copy), not lorem ipsum or generic "Unlock your potential". `[VISUAL]` (NN/G #3, Impeccable generic-claims rules)

Visual craft
6. Pick typography deliberately for the product's character: not a default (Inter/Roboto/system) just because it is default; contrast between display and text faces, real weight/size contrast (Anthropic suggests 3x+ size jumps, extreme weights); one face used decisively beats a timid pair; but do not swap into the new cliché (italic serif display + cream palette is also flagged by Impeccable). Verify LT glyph rendering. `[VISUAL]` + `[CODE]` for "font outside tokens"
7. Hierarchy by subtraction: de-emphasise siblings, vary weight/colour not just size, reduce borders (use space, shade, shadow), start with too much white space then remove. `[VISUAL]` (Refactoring UI)
8. Intentional depth: one light source, layered two-part shadows, colour-matched shadows; elevation increases offset and blur and lowers opacity; no hairline border + wide shadow doubling. `[BOTH]` (Comeau, Refactoring UI, Vercel, Impeccable)
9. Colour with a point of view: one accent doing primary-action work, tinted neutrals with a consistent temperature, semantic colours reserved for meaning; avoid the AI palette (purple/cyan gradients, glow-on-dark, gradient text, beige default). `[BOTH]` (Refactoring UI, Impeccable, Anthropic)
10. Spend expressiveness where it earns attention: make the one key action larger/higher-contrast/better placed (M3 Expressive: 4x faster to spot) while keeping familiar patterns and visible labels; be bold only where function is not harmed. `[VISUAL]` (Google research)
11. Vary rhythm: unequal spacing that encodes grouping, mixed section layouts, not identical icon-heading-text card grids, no nested cards, no eyebrow-label-over-every-heading, no pills over headlines, no hero-metric template unless it carries meaning. `[BOTH]` (Impeccable, NN/G)
12. Imagery: "good photos or none"; treat text-over-image with overlay; no placeholder circles-and-blocks illustrations; custom icons or a single consistent set at native size (don't scale 24 px icons to 72). `[VISUAL]` (Refactoring UI, Impeccable)
13. Copy is part of the design: specific, active voice, no "supercharge/world-class", no forced "Not X. Y." contrasts, em-dash discipline, no AI-sparkle decoration. `[BOTH]` (Impeccable, Vercel, NN/G State of UX)

Motion and interaction
14. Motion budget by frequency of use (Emil's table) with a stated purpose for each animation (spatial consistency, state, feedback, explanation); zero animation on keyboard-driven, high-frequency actions. `[VISUAL]` (Emil, Rauno, NN/G)
15. Signature micro-details: press feedback (`scale(0.97)`), origin-aware popovers, stagger 30–80 ms, enter slower than exit, asymmetric deliberate-press vs snappy-release, tooltip delay then instant, custom ease-out curve rather than CSS defaults. `[BOTH]` (Emil)
16. Interruptible, gesture-honest interactions: action fires on swipe distance only if non-destructive; destructive triggers on release; apply scale delta immediately; velocity-based dismissal; friction at boundaries. `[VISUAL]` (Rauno, Emil)
17. Match motion personality to the product's mood (a wedding invitation can be softer and slower than a dashboard, still under NN/G's 100–500 ms envelope). `[VISUAL]` (Emil "cohesion", Comeau)

Process
18. Critique loop with fresh eyes and a different reviewer: score hierarchy / clarity / craft, rank issues P0–P3, output a Before | After | Why table; run the deterministic detectors first, then judgement review; look at it in slow motion and next day; test at 375 px, laptop, ultra-wide, on a real phone. Don't trust fidelity as quality. `[BOTH]` (Impeccable, Emil, Vercel, NN/G)
19. Treat detector hits as prompts to look closer, not bans: a deliberate, documented choice (e.g. a side-stripe used for real status) can stay. Intent beats checklist. `[VISUAL]` (Impeccable caveat, Saarinen, NN/G taste)
20. Platform effects (Liquid Glass, M3 springs) are borrowed only for their principle (functional layer above content, key action prominence), never as a theme: NN/G documented real legibility and target-size failures from glass. `[VISUAL]` (Apple HIG, NN/G, Google)

### 3C. Tensions and decisions the skill must make explicit

- Motion duration: Emil says UI under 300 ms; NN/G 100–500; Apple/Material allow longer for large spatial moves. Proposed rule: ≤300 ms default, ≤500 ms for modal/drawer/page-scale only.
- Contrast: WCAG 2.2 ratio is the legal/testable gate; APCA is better perceptually but not yet a standard; require both, WCAG as blocking.
- Line length: Butterick 45–90, Bringhurst 45–75 (66 ideal), Impeccable 65–75: adopt 45–75ch, cap ~66–70ch.
- Standards vs distinctiveness: NN/G wants Jakob's-Law consistency for patterns (nav, forms, checkout) but a distinctive visual identity; distinctiveness belongs in type, colour, imagery, motion, layout rhythm, not in re-inventing controls (Google's playlist study shows the cost).
- Detector vs taste: Impeccable's own "tasteful default" example shows that mechanically avoiding purple/Inter yields a new template; the brief and the single organising idea must come first.
- Density: Refactoring UI says generous white space by default; dashboards/tables may densify deliberately (and keep ≥24/44 px targets).
- Gaps in my evidence: no numeric source read for Material 48 dp; Radix APCA step guarantees unverified; Bringhurst/Refactoring UI read only through digests; Jenny Wen interview not accessible; NN/G AI-in-UX survey only excerpted.
