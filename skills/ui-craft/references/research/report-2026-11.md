# How AI should make UI/UX: what the best guidance says (2026-11-25)

Research report. It is the groundwork for a skill that makes our agents produce original, crafted interfaces instead of AI slop. Five research tracks read about 150 primary sources: Vercel; the model labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google); community anti-slop skills; research evidence and benchmarks; and human craft authorities. The raw notes, with every URL, are in [sources/](sources/). This file is the synthesis and the recommendation.

Three load-bearing claims were re-checked against their primary pages before writing: Anthropic's "house style" admission, Vercel's 39-vs-91 eval, and the Design Creativity Bench numbers. All three match word for word.

---

## 1. The short version

1. **Slop is the model's average, and it is very consistent.** Asked the same brief, a model picked the same theme 100% of the time [04: S28]. 73 different businesses built in Lovable came out as about 12 effective designs [04: S35]. Models agree with each other on design far more than humans do: originality 0.59 between models vs 0.76 with a human in the pair [04: S21, verified]. Running the prompt again does not make it vary.
2. **Ban-lists do not cure it. They move the model to the next default.** This is now the official position.
   - Anthropic's own Opus 4.8 docs: *"Generic instructions ('don't use cream,' 'make it clean and minimal') tend to shift the model to a different fixed palette rather than producing variety"* (verified).
   - The record shows it happening. The 2025 cure was "ban Inter, use Space Grotesk / Playfair". In 2026 those fonts and the cream-serif-terracotta look are themselves the top tells, and Anthropic now names its own accent colour `#D97757` as one.
3. **What works is deciding the design before code, from outside the model's average:**
   - ground it in the subject's real world;
   - name a specific reference ("a 1970s university lecture handout"), not adjectives ("modern, clean");
   - generate several genuinely different directions;
   - pick one by a rule or by a human, not by the model's taste;
   - write it down as a direction contract;
   - check it against "what would the default build be?".

   Every serious source converged on this independently: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google's DESIGN.md philosophy, Vercel, Impeccable, Hallmark and Refero.
4. **Tell-lists still matter, but as detection, not instruction.** Naming the known failures and checking for them mechanically cut known failures 57% in Vercel's eval (39 vs 91; n = 6 pages, verified, with honest caveats). The list goes stale, so date it and review it with each model generation.
5. **Quality comes from rendering, operating and having someone else critique it.**
   - 2–3 screenshot critique rounds gave +10.8% to +17.8% [04: S29].
   - Judging after clicking through the app repaired 75.5% of broken requirements vs 55.4% from code alone [04: S23].
   - A separate, sceptical evaluator beats self-grading [02: Anthropic harness post].
6. **Don't trust an AI judge on taste.**
   - LLM judges pick the busier page 83% of the time, where people do 63% [04: S13].
   - A Claude judge favoured Anthropic-made pages by about 10 points [04: S26].
   - Rubric scores matched a designer only 35% of the time. Order-swapped side-by-side comparison matched 63%.
   - Use judges for defects, and use humans or the brief for direction.
7. **There is a hard quality floor no style excuses.** WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, focus, 24/44 px targets, every state designed, no overflow, labels on inputs. Low-contrast text is on 83.9% of the top million home pages [04: S46]. The floor is cheap to check in code.
8. **Our product is in the highest-risk zone.** Impeccable's Claude-specific notes say warm, bookish, family and child-facing subjects come out as *"cream grounds, serif display with italic accents, and lamplight"*. Wedding invitations are exactly that. Our own chosen brand (Italianno, wine and cream) is a pinned owner decision and stays. But the skill must actively push the invitation designs away from that prior.

## 2. Why AI design converges (the evidence)

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Models give the same theme for the same brief | 100% same theme; a "pick a spec first" step raised it to 2.6–2.9 options | [04: S28], industry preprint |
| Models resemble each other more than humans do | originality 0.59 (model-model) vs 0.76 (human-model) | Design Creativity Bench, preprint, verified |
| Changing the product domain barely changes the design | creative range 0.58 for models vs 0.90 for humans | same |
| Designs are competent though | > 90% of acceptance criteria met by every model | same |
| Real users with one tool | 73 briefs → about 12 distinct designs; 88% fell in 6 clusters | [04: S35] |
| Layout converges more than colour | ~25% of the tools' stated design rationales were never implemented | Design Theater, AIES 2026 [04: S36] |
| Defaults go out of fashion and models keep making them | beige-serif "tasteful" pages lost voter preference Jan→Oct 2026 while models kept producing them | Design Arena analysis [04: S13] |

Causes are several: training data dominated by "safe" sites (Anthropic calls it *distributional convergence*), preference training that rewards the typical answer (Verbalized Sampling, ICML 2026), and convergence already present in base models. The popular "Tailwind's `bg-indigo-500` caused purple AI" story comes from Adam Wathan's 2025-08-07 tweet. That tweet was a joke, and nothing measured it as the cause. Colour is the *least* converged axis; layout and structure are the most [04]. **Banning purple treats the most visible symptom of a structural problem.**

NN/g's studies (2025-10, 2025-12, 2026-11) give the UX side of slop, which goes deeper than looks [05]:
- "Frankenstein layouts": clutter, the same information shown twice, content in an illogical order, and big containers holding one trivial number.
- Even with detailed prompts: weak grouping, poor contrast and inconsistent spacing.
- Wrong patterns from vague nouns, such as a "profile page" built as a social profile.
- A generic shadcn/Tailwind look. Their verdict: *"good from afar, but far from good"*.
- Giving the generator *synthesised decisions* beat giving it raw user comments by 8.4 points (p < 0.001).

## 4. The method that works, ranked by evidence

| # | Practice | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Decide in writing before code.** Brief: product surface, context of use (who, when, what decision), constraints. Then a spec: 4–6 named colours with roles, type roles, layout concept with an ASCII sketch, alignment, principles. No code until it exists. | NN/g: detailed prompts consistently better, synthesised decisions +8.4 pts; Anthropic docs: "the model follows explicit specs precisely"; Vercel v0 brief template; leaked v0 makes it mandatory | Moderate, universal consensus |
| 2 | **Ground it in the subject's world and a specific reference.** List ~7 objects, places, rituals and materials the audience knows, spanning at least 3 material families; ask "what did this look like before the web?". | Anthropic Gen 2–3; Google PHILOSOPHY; Impeccable; our own mood-board-creator "vernaculars" method and lessons | Moderate, universal |
| 3 | **Diverge outside the model, then select by rule or human.** Generate 3–5 directions that differ in structure (topology, density, type, imagery), not just colour. Pick by owner, by external seed, or "furthest from default", never "the model's favourite". Log what was used so the next build differs. | Verbalized Sampling 1.6–2.1× diversity (text); UI: 1 → 2.9 options, preference neutral; Anthropic 4 directions; Claude Design, Lovable, Refero: 3; Impeccable and Hallmark dealers + logs | Strong for variety, **neutral for quality**: verify quality separately |
| 4 | **Default-collision check.** Write down the build a no-guidance model would make; revise anything in the plan that matches it. "Mirror test": would a neighbouring prompt in this category get this combination? Two-altitude check: guessable from category alone, or from category plus the things we said to avoid. | Anthropic ("work through a similar prompt"), Hallmark, Impeccable, Vercel ("name the obvious layout, reject it unless the material earns it") | Moderate |
| 5 | **One memorable thing, everything else quiet; then a removal pass.** "Can any surface, border, pill, icon, label or section be removed without losing meaning?" Busy (remove) is diagnosed separately from loud (reduce intensity). | Anthropic, Vercel design.md, Hallmark signature; guards against the opposite failure, a "sterile anti-design template" | Moderate |
| 6 | **Real content and material; never invent facts.** No fake metrics, testimonials, logos, counts or prices; label demo data. | SkillGild: 4 of 5 skill runs invented product facts; Hallmark "facts are never inferred" | Strong (it is a defect) |
| 7 | **Render, operate, critique for 2–3 rounds, by a separate reviewer.** Shots at phone and desktop width, click and keyboard through, critique as concrete edits ("element X: problem → change"), keep the *best* round, not the latest. | +10.8–17.8% over 3 cycles; interaction judge 75.5% vs 55.4%; designer edits beat ratings (Apple CHI 2026); Anthropic: middle iterations were sometimes best | Moderate-strong for quality, none for originality |
| 8 | **Deterministic detector for tells and floor, outside the model.** Computed-style and DOM checks; findings are "reasons to look closer"; waivers need a written reason. | Vercel 57% fewer known failures; WebAIM failure classes; Impeccable, Hallmark, Krebs (16 checks over 1,590 pages) | Strong for what it covers |
| 9 | **Persistent design context.** PRODUCT.md (audience, scene, constraints, anti-references) and DESIGN.md in Google format: prose first, tokens as a bounded vocabulary. New styles enter only through the system. | Google, Vercel, Impeccable, Figma Make, Balyasnikov; Vercel: "agents kept inventing their own typography, spacing, and layout, so we took those decisions away" | Moderate, universal |
| 10 | **Paste the pattern spec for interactive components** (WAI-ARIA APG text and states list). | 12/12 vs 6/12 correct behaviour (small pilot) | Moderate |
| 11 | **Mode by surface.** Persuade (landing, invitation) gets full commitment. Operate (admin, tools) gets precision and restraint, a working surface first, no hero. | OpenAI GPT-5.5, Impeccable modes, Vercel product-design | Consensus |

## 5. The current tell list (as of 2026-11; detection, not instruction)

Use these to *check* output. Each item is allowed when the brief, a real product truth or an owner pin earns it, with a written reason. Re-date this list with each model generation.

**First generation (2024–25), still caught:** purple/indigo-to-blue gradients and the framework's default swatch; gradient text; glows, aurora blobs, orbs, glassmorphism; Inter/Roboto/system as display type; emoji as icons; centred hero followed by three identical icon cards; fake stat strips.

**Second generation (2026, the live battleground):**
- Cream/ivory ground (~`#F4F1EA`) + high-contrast serif display + terracotta/clay accent (~`#D97757`); token names like `--paper`, `--cream`, `--parchment`. Flagged by Anthropic, OpenAI, Impeccable, Hallmark and Refero.
- A single accented word in a headline (italic, colour or swapped face); italic serif display as a system.
- Near-black + one acid-green or vermilion accent; tinted near-black `#0B0B0B`/`#111` standing in for black.
- Broadsheet hairlines, zero radius, dense newspaper columns by default.
- Tracked ALL-CAPS eyebrow or kicker above every heading; badge pill above the H1; labels above content that add nothing.
- Numbered 01 / 02 / 03 section markers when the content is not a sequence.
- The SaaS card kit: identical rounded cards, one radius everywhere, the same `rgba(0,0,0,.1)` shadow, nested cards, coloured side stripes, icon tiles above headings, a 1 px border plus a wide soft shadow ("ghost card").
- The hero-metric block (big number, small label, stat strip, gradient accent).
- Template chrome: `A · B · C` meta strings, `WORD — fragment` labels, `→` glued to links, monospace "for tech" on small labels, pulsing green status dots, blinking cursors, marquees.
- Fade-and-slide-up on every section; hover lift on every card.
- Fonts that were the 2025 cure: Space Grotesk, Playfair, Fraunces, Cormorant, Instrument Serif, Geist, Satoshi, Outfit, DM Sans/Serif, IBM Plex, all when used by reflex. Technique: have the model name its top three fonts, then discard all three.

**Copy tells:** em-dash habit; "Not X. Y." fragments; question-then-answer reveals ("The result? …"); elevate / seamless / unlock / supercharge; invented specifics ("Batch No. 047", "6:12 a.m."); one warm, wry "house voice" for every client; page text narrating the design process or explaining the UI; "Scroll to explore".

**Model-specific habits** [03: Bakaus]: Claude uses italic serifs, eyebrow kickers and pulsing green dots. GPT crushes display tracking to `-0.06em`. Gemini scales images on hover. Keep per-model rules separate; stacking them regresses other models.

**UX tells (NN/g):** duplicated information; secondary things made most prominent; big containers holding one trivial number; illogical content order; wrong pattern from a vague noun; missing empty, error and loading states.

## 6. Quality floor and craft ceiling

**Floor: always, checkable in code.** A failure here is a defect, not a style choice.

- **Contrast:** text ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1, UI and state indicators ≥ 3:1, in light and dark (WCAG 2.2 AA is the blocking gate; APCA is an extra check, because WCAG 3 is still a draft).
- **Keyboard:** `:focus-visible` on everything, never hidden under sticky bars. Native elements first; dialogs trap and return focus; tabs, menus and combobox follow APG.
- **Targets and layout:** targets ≥ 24 px, ≥ 44 px on touch. No layout break at 320 px or 200% text zoom; no horizontal overflow; no clipped popovers; images carry their dimensions.
- **Motion:** honour `prefers-reduced-motion`; nothing auto-moves for more than 5 s without a pause control; animate transform/opacity only; never `transition: all`; no ease-in on UI; 100–300 ms (≤ 500 ms for modals and drawers); content visible even if an animation fails.
- **Forms:** labels above fields, never placeholder-only; correct `type`, `inputmode` and `autocomplete`; mobile input ≥ 16 px; errors next to the field, with focus on the first error; never block paste or zoom.
- **States:** every interactive element has hover, focus, active, disabled and loading states. Every data view has empty, sparse, long, loading and error states. No dead ends. Destructive actions are named Verb + Noun and confirmed or undoable. Feedback within 400 ms.
- **Type:** measure 45–75 ch; body 16–20 px at line-height 1.4–1.6; balanced headings; tabular numerals in comparisons; real quotes and ellipses. For us: Lithuanian diacritics checked in the actual font (we already have `lt-specimen.mjs`).
- **One system:** one type scale, one spacing scale, one radius family (child radius ≤ parent), one shadow scale, colour roles as tokens; no one-off values.

**Ceiling: what separates designed from generated** (mostly needs visual judgement):
- **One organising idea.** It must be specific enough that *"an unrelated product could not use it unchanged"*.
- **Hierarchy by subtraction.** Grey-scale first; de-emphasise siblings; space and shade before borders; exactly one primary action.
- **Typography as the voice.** Chosen from the subject's world, used decisively, with real size and weight contrast.
- **Colour with a strategy decided first.** Restrained, committed, full or drenched; neutrals with one temperature; light or dark decided by a one-sentence physical scene (who, where, under what light), not by category.
- **Intentional depth.** One light source, layered and colour-matched shadows, concentric radii, optical alignment to ±1 px.
- **Rhythm.** Unequal spacing that encodes grouping, and different section silhouettes for different jobs.
- **Imagery.** "Good photos or none"; real material, never fake CSS illustrations.
- **Theme the browser surfaces you didn't draw.** Selection colour, caret, scrollbars, focus ring, underline offset. Impeccable calls this *"the cheapest signal that a page was built rather than assembled"*.
- **Motion budgeted by frequency of use.** None on actions people repeat many times a day; one authored moment; press feedback `scale(0.97)`; popovers that grow from their trigger; exits faster than entrances.
- **Copy is design material.** Specific, active verbs, the same name for an action through the whole flow, errors that say how to fix the problem.

## 7. How to evaluate (and what not to trust)

Cheapest first:
1. **Deterministic checks.** Floor plus tells. They are not opinion; run them every round.
2. **Behaviour.** Operate it: every control, every state. Model-written rationales are not evidence, since about 25% are never implemented.
3. **Pairwise visual review.** Put the result next to the "default build" for the same brief. Order-swapped, counted only if the same pick wins both ways, by a judge from a different model family than the generator. It is good for coarse choices only: 57–70% agreement with people.
4. **Human look** at rendered screens, including scroll bars and a recorded motion pass (our existing rule).

Bake in the judge pitfalls:
- AI judges reward busier screens (83% vs 63% for people), and rewording the judge prompt barely helps.
- Judges favour their own lab's output by about 10 points.
- A panel of judges from different labs shares the same bias, so it does not average out.
- Absolute rubric grades are the weakest form (35% agreement with a designer).
- Criteria wording steers output: never write "museum quality" or "award-winning" into criteria.
- Arena rank is not originality. Even the most original model sits 0.12 below humans.

**Eval harness for the skill itself** (Vercel's method):
- 5–7 frozen scenarios from our product, for example: couple landing, invitation, guest RSVP, RSVP confirmation, admin list, email.
- Generate each with and without the skill, first attempt only, and run a blind A/B.
- Record every correction against the run that produced it, and land it in the narrowest place: prose, shared CSS, or a check.
- Re-run the affected scenarios.

## 8. What does not work (folk wisdom, no evidence or contrary evidence)

- Ban-lists of fonts and colours as the *generation* instruction. They shift the model to a new fixed default (Anthropic docs; the 2025 → 2026 history).
- "Be creative / unique / don't hold back" and "act as a senior designer". No measured effect. Raising temperature mixes aesthetic noise with code noise.
- More reasoning effort for one-shot pages: 21% vs 15% broken pages at max vs high effort [04: S13].
- A self-graded rubric as the only gate: lenient by design.
- LLM judges scoring originality or taste: not validated anywhere.
- Style lookup tables (category → style), cloned famous-brand DESIGN.md files, fixed template kits. Each converges across users.
- "Average" several references into a safe middle. Pick one dominant reference and keep its sharp traits.
- Copying platform effects (Liquid Glass) as a theme.

## 9. Tensions the skill must settle

| Tension | Resolution I recommend |
|---|---|
| Bans fail vs. naming tells cuts failures 57% | Tells live in the **detector and the critique**, each paired with a positive move, never as the main generation prompt. Our mood-board-creator v8 already dropped global bans; this keeps that and adds detection with waivers. |
| Restraint (Vercel: stillness, monochrome) vs. expression (invitations need warmth and ornament) | Mode by surface: invitation and storefront designs = full commitment; admin = restraint. Vercel's bans on ornament, pills and eyebrows are its own brand choices, not universal rules. |
| Motion: none (ibelick) / one moment (Anthropic) / 2–3 per page (OpenAI 5.4) | Default to one authored moment plus motion that answers user actions; budget by frequency (Emil). Invitations may earn a richer opening moment. |
| Variety vs. quality | Diverging buys variety only. Quality comes from the floor, render-critique rounds and human selection. Measure the two separately. |
| WCAG vs. APCA | WCAG 2.2 AA blocks; APCA warns. |
| Our brand cream vs. the cream-serif tell | Owner pins win ("the brief's own words always win"). The tell applies to *unconsidered* use, so new invitation designs must reach cream only through a stated reason. |
| English-centric rules (Title Case, `&`, curly-quote conventions) | Re-check for Lithuanian: quotes are „…“, sentence case, diacritics. Vercel's Title Case is Vercel-specific. |

## 10. What this means for us

**What we already have that matches the research.**
- `frontend-design` v1.1.1 is installed and is the current Gen 3.
- mood-board-creator already holds several of these ideas:
  - the vernacular / "visual world" method;
  - a lesson owner feedback confirmed: trend skins failed, while product essence plus a quality bar from real reference-app screenshots won;
  - the UX layer comes before visuals;
  - fresh independent critics, and no global bans.
- The purchased designs (V06 Cianotipija, V07 Karpiniai, V09 Viršelis, V10 Kadras) are already grounded in real-world vernaculars. That is exactly the method the research endorses.

**What the research adds that we don't have yet:**
1. A dated **tell list plus quality floor as a deterministic detector**: a Playwright script on chrome-headless-shell reading computed styles at 390 and 1440 px.
2. **External divergence with a history log**: a project file of directions, fonts and palettes already used, so new designs must differ on several structural axes.
3. A **written direction contract** gate before code, plus the default-collision and mirror checks.
4. **Blind two-agent critique**: a design reviewer who has not seen the detector output, plus the detector itself. The lead weaves the two.
5. A **frozen-scenario A/B eval** for the skill itself.
6. A **learn-taste loop**: each owner correction becomes a rule with its why, at class level, with a lint check where possible.
7. A **trigger line in AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md**. Vercel measured skills failing to trigger in 56% of cases without one.

**Proposed skill shape** (a blueprint, not built yet):
```
skill: <name>/
  SKILL.md            router, under ~100 lines; modes: direct | build | critique | harden
  references/
    direction.md      world derivation, 3–5 directions, selection rule, direction contract, default-collision check
    tells.md          dated tell list (§5) + per-model blocks + positive move for each
    floor.md          §6 floor with numbers; Lithuanian specifics
    critique.md       reviewer brief, edit-form findings, judge pitfalls, refine-or-pivot
    motion.md, copy.md
  scripts/
    detect.mjs        tells + floor from computed styles (chrome-headless-shell, closed in finally)
    shot.mjs          390 / 1440, scroll bars visible, optional motion recording
per project:
  PRODUCT.md, DESIGN.md (Google format, prose first), .design-log.json (directions used)
```

**Weighed against cost** (where weighing changed the best practice):
- **Not adopting:**
  - Impeccable's full harness (7.6× tokens, telemetry, launches system Chrome);
  - always-on PostToolUse hooks until the detector is calibrated (run it at build end and in critique first);
  - OpenAI's image-first concept pipeline, except as an optional mode for a flagship page;
  - embedding-distance "originality" scores, which are validated as measurement but not as a target.
- **Keeping the always-on layer small:** Anthropic's skill gets most of the benefit at 1.6×.

## 11. Decisions for you before the skill is built

**A. Build our own skill, or install Impeccable or Hallmark?**
- Downsides of installing: cost (Impeccable 7.6×), telemetry, launching system Chrome (breaks our rule), marketing-site bias, and their taste replacing ours.
- Common practice: teams fork Anthropic's skill and add their own context files.
- Simplest: our own small skill plus a detector script, borrowing their ideas.
- Worth it: yes. The ideas transfer; the harnesses don't fit.
- **Recommendation: build our own**, extending mood-board-creator's method rather than starting a parallel system.

**B. Who picks the direction: you, or a random dealer?**
- Downsides of a dealer: random picks can be off-brand, and dealt variety showed no quality gain.
- Downsides of the owner picking: it costs your time every round.
- Common practice: 3–4 directions, human picks (Anthropic, Claude Design, Lovable, Refero).
- Simplest: the agent proposes 3 directions that differ in structure; you pick; the log prevents repeats.
- **Recommendation: you pick for storefront and invitation designs; the agent picks "furthest from default" for small surfaces.**

**C. Where does the skill apply?**
- Downsides of applying it everywhere: the admin would get "creative" pressure it doesn't need.
- Common practice: modes by surface.
- **Recommendation: full method for storefront and invitation designs; floor plus Vercel guidelines only for the admin** (it stays plain shadcn, as decided).

**Suggested next step:** turn this report into the skill: router, references, `detect.mjs`. Then run the frozen-scenario A/B on 5 of our surfaces to prove it changes output before it goes into the build cycles.
